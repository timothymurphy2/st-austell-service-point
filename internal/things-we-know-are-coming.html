A place for real life

The things we know are coming

Some expenses are predictable. That does not make them easy when the weekly money is already spoken for.

The costs of childhood

James Dyer described parents in Gilfach Goch planning for uniforms, Christmas, holidays and their children’s activities through a local credit union outlet. Items other households might call basic could still take deliberate preparation.

The value of being in control

Helen Bishop described the pride of paying with money she had put aside herself and understanding what was available after bills. Her account was about control as much as an account balance.

Close enough to use

James explained that the community centre outlet made the possibility visible to families already using the building. A few miles to another town may be a barrier without a car or affordable transport.

Ordinary costs deserve an ordinary way to prepare.

Local volunteers are working to make a route to Kernow Credit Union easier to find in St Austell. The Service Point is still being developed.

These situations draw on the experiences and observations in The Guardian’s 2012 film Credit Unions: Money with Morals, which followed Dragon Savers in Wales. They illustrate questions for St Austell; they do not describe services already operating here or guarantee a loan or outcome.
