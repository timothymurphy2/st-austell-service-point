A place for real life

Another possibility, close to home

Sometimes the first need is simply to speak with a person and understand what choices are available.

A decision with a person behind it

Christina Stoneman explained that Dragon Savers considered credit information, but its loan officers made and stood by personal decisions. That describes her credit union in the film; it is not a promise about Kernow Credit Union’s decisions.

A familiar place

James Dyer saw the importance of an outlet people could encounter in a community centre. Its presence mattered to those for whom even a short journey elsewhere was difficult.

What John Harris saw

Harris contrasted large, impersonal financial providers and expensive lenders with the different possibility emerging through local credit unions. He also saw responsibility and community connection in the way Dragon Savers worked.

The human first step

Helen Bishop said the credit union helped her understand her debts and take greater control of household money. A welcoming first conversation can be the start of finding out what may be possible.

A local place makes an alternative visible.

Local volunteers are working to make a route to Kernow Credit Union easier to find in St Austell. The Service Point is still being developed.

These situations draw on the experiences and observations in The Guardian’s 2012 film Credit Unions: Money with Morals, which followed Dragon Savers in Wales. They illustrate questions for St Austell; they do not describe services already operating here or guarantee a loan or outcome.
