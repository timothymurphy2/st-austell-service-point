A place for real life

When something breaks

An unexpected bill can arrive in a week when there is no spare money. What matters then is being able to find out what choices exist.

A few hundred pounds can matter

Christina Stoneman, who managed Dragon Savers, described people who occasionally needed to borrow a few hundred pounds. She spoke about the importance and responsibility of a human decision. Mainstream providers were often uninterested in amounts this small, while easily found alternatives could be costly.

The quick offer has a longer story

Helen Bishop described how the attraction of cash when she wanted something for her daughter became several loans and repayments that left little for essentials. Her experience shows why a family deserves time and clear information before making a decision.

A question worth asking early

What would your family do if an appliance failed next month? Knowing where a local credit union is, and how to ask about its options, can begin before the emergency.

Another door should be visible before it is urgently needed.

Local volunteers are working to make a route to Kernow Credit Union easier to find in St Austell. The Service Point is still being developed.

These situations draw on the experiences and observations in The Guardian’s 2012 film Credit Unions: Money with Morals, which followed Dragon Savers in Wales. They illustrate questions for St Austell; they do not describe services already operating here or guarantee a loan or outcome.
